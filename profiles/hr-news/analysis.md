# Evaluation goal

Evaluate the importance of timely recruiting, talent-market and HR-technology news for readers who follow recruiting, talent acquisition, the labor and talent market, HR technology, and employment policy, with particular interest in Korea and in how AI is changing hiring and talent search.

# Scoring rubric

- **9-10: Major shift.** Industry-changing events: a major platform (LinkedIn, Indeed, Workday, Saramin, Wanted, JobKorea, etc.) changes products, data access, or pricing in a way that reshapes hiring; large funding or M&A in recruiting or talent-data tech; landmark employment data showing a clear turn in the talent market.
- **7-8: High value.** Concrete developments worth prompt attention: notable AI recruiting or sourcing product launches, meaningful hiring or layoff moves by bellwether companies, survey or data releases with specific numbers about hiring demand, candidate behavior, or skills, and changes in how leading companies recruit or assess talent.
- **5-6: Interesting.** Incremental product updates, smaller funding rounds, regional hiring stories with some generalizable signal, or practical cases from recruiting teams.
- **3-4: Low priority.** Routine announcements, generic hiring-season stories, local events, or articles with little specific information.
- **0-2: Noise.** Job postings, advertorials, recruitment fairs listings, press releases without substance, celebrity or sports news, or off-topic material.

# Evaluation guidance

Prefer items with concrete numbers, named companies, and clear mechanisms. Reward items that change how candidates are found, evaluated, or hired, and items about talent data, sourcing, and AI in recruiting. Korean items deserve equal weight to global ones when they carry real signal. Many Korean outlets republish the same press release; judge the underlying event, not the outlet. Do not reward exaggerated headlines or vendor self-promotion.

Use three to five specific topic tags.
